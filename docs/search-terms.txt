HOW TO SCREEN CANDIDATES: 

Field 
	What to establish 

Publication and target finding
	Which published contrast,  association, interaction, or time-course result will be rechecked?

Dataset identity 
	Repository URL, DOI/version, and  whether other papers reuse the same participants

Data level 
	Continuous raw samples; raw trial  segments; preprocessed time series; trial summaries; group summaries

Timing and task metadata 
	Timestamps, event markers, trial IDs, conditions, stimulus timing, and response timing

Acquisition 
	Tracker, sampling rate, recorded eye(s), pupil area versus diameter, and measurement units

Original workflow 
	Preprocessing settings, exclusion rules, baseline interval, analysis window, model, and code

Context and confounds 
	Luminance information, gaze position, blinks, missingness, and relevant task differences

Reuse feasibility 
	Accessible files, documentation, usage conditions, conversion needs, and unresolved questions

Complementary strategies

1. Citation chasing. Take the key seed papers  (Mathôt & Vilotijević 2023; Steinhauer et al. 2022; Fink et al. 2024,  which has a table of cognitive processes linked to pupil size; Kret &  Sjak-Shie 2019; Joshi & Gold 2020; Mathôt 2018; van der Wel & van  Steenbergen 2018) and run forward and backward citation searches in  OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, or Scopus.
2. Meta-research angle. Search for pupil* AND  (reanalysis OR re-analysis OR reproducib* OR replicat* OR multiverse OR  "analytic flexibility" OR "preprocessing choices" OR  "forking paths"). Also run pupil* AND "meta-analysis" so you know what already  exists. Studies that have already been re-analyzed or replicated are  useful comparison points.
3. Preprints. Run the same strings in  PsyArXiv, bioRxiv, and OSF Preprints. Preprints often link their data when  the journal version doesn’t.
4. OSF Registries. Search pupil* for preregistered pupil  studies, which are more likely to have open data.
5. Journals with data-sharing  requirements or badges. Filter for these (e.g., eLife, PLOS ONE, Scientific Reports,  Nature Communications, Behavior Research Methods). Check each journal’s  current policy before relying on this.
6. Author contact for promising studies where  the paper says “available on request.”
